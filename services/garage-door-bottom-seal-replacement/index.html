The bottom seal is the only thing standing between your garage and whatever is on the driveway: rain, snowmelt, dust, leaves, mice and cold air. When it hardens, cracks or tears away, the door also stops sealing evenly, which lets water sit against the bottom panel and rust it out.

Bottom seal replacement in your area is a small job with a big effect on comfort and on the life of the door. We match the seal profile to your door — not every door takes the same gasket — and check the bottom panel and apron for the gap that caused the wear.

What bottom seal involves

The bottom seal is a rubber or vinyl gasket that runs along the bottom edge of the lowest panel, compressing against the floor when the door closes. There are several profiles: C-channel slide-in, bulb, U-shape, T-end and blade seals. The right profile depends on the door's retainer.

What is included

  • Old seal removal and retainer cleaning
  • Correct profile matched to your door
  • New seal cut to width and fitted
  • Bottom panel and apron inspection
  • Adjustment of the door's down limit
  • Optional threshold or apron seal advice

Signs you need this service

  • Daylight visible under the closed door
  • Water pooling at the garage floor after rain
  • Cold drafts or dust blowing in around the bottom
  • The seal is cracked, brittle or torn away
  • Mice or insects getting in under the door
  • The bottom panel is rusting along its lower edge

How the visit works

  1. 1

    Identify the profile

    The technician checks the retainer type and door width so the replacement seal fits properly.

  2. 2

    Remove the old seal

    The worn gasket is pulled out and the retainer is cleaned of dirt and rust.

  3. 3

    Fit the new seal

    The new seal is cut to length and slid or clipped into the retainer.

  4. 4

    Adjust and test

    The door's closed position is adjusted so the seal compresses evenly without binding.

Bottom Seal questions

How often should a bottom seal be replaced?

Every 3–5 years in most climates, sooner if the door faces direct sun or the floor drains poorly.

Can I just buy a universal seal?

Universal seals fit some retainers but leave gaps on others. Matching the profile to your door is what makes the seal watertight.

Will a new seal stop water coming in?

It stops water that comes in under the door. If water runs toward the garage from the driveway, an apron or threshold seal is the next step.

Does it help with cold?

Yes. A tight bottom seal is one of the biggest differences you can make to a garage that sits over or beside living space.

Will a new seal stop mice?

It closes the biggest entry gap. For a full fix, the perimeter seals and the door-to-frame gaps should be checked as well.
