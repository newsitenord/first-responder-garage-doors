A door goes off track when a roller jumps the rail, a track bends, or one side of the door lifts while the other stays put. Forcing the opener at that point turns a modest repair into bent panels, snapped cables and a door that has to be replaced.

Off-track repairs in your area are about finding out why it happened. Our your county technicians reset or replace the affected track, check the rollers, brackets and cables on both sides, and rebalance the door so it does not happen again next week.

What off-track repair involves

Off-track repair means returning the door's rollers to the vertical and horizontal tracks, straightening or replacing bent track sections, and correcting whatever caused the derailment — a loose bracket, a worn roller, an impact, or a door that was lifted while the opener was still engaged.

What is included

  • Door secured and opener disconnected before work
  • Track straightening or section replacement
  • Roller, hinge and bracket inspection
  • Cable and spring condition check
  • Door rebalance and travel adjustment
  • Safety test and full open-close cycle

Signs you need this service

  • The door is visibly crooked in the opening
  • A roller has popped out of the track
  • The door binds, scrapes or stops partway
  • The track is bent after a vehicle or impact
  • The door makes a heavy clunk when it starts
  • One side of the door sits lower than the other

How the visit works

  1. 1

    Stop using the door

    An off-track door should never be run with the opener — that is what bends panels.

  2. 2

    Assess the damage

    The technician checks tracks, rollers, hinges, cables and brackets on both sides of the door.

  3. 3

    Reset and replace

    Rollers are reseated, bent track sections are straightened or replaced and the door is brought back to plumb.

  4. 4

    Rebalance

    The door is balanced with the opener released, then the opener is reconnected and calibrated.

Off-Track Repair questions

Can I just push the door back on the track?

In rare cases the door can be lifted back on, but the underlying cause is still there and the door is likely to derail again. The rollers, brackets and track need to be checked properly.

How did my door come off track?

The usual causes are a worn roller, a loose track bracket, a small impact, a broken cable or an unbalanced door that lifts unevenly.

Is it an emergency?

If the door is hanging or blocking access, yes — call for emergency dispatch. If the door is closed and stable, it can wait for a scheduled appointment.

Will my panels be damaged?

Not always. If the door is stopped quickly, panels usually survive. Panels bend when the opener keeps running against a jammed door.

How long does the repair take?

Most realignments take 45–90 minutes; replacing a bent track section can take a little longer.
