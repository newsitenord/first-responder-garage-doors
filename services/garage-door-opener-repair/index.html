When an opener fails it is not always the motor. In our experience it is just as often a stripped gear, a tired capacitor, a misaligned safety sensor, a remote that lost its programming or a door that has become too heavy for the opener to move. Replacing the unit before finding the real cause is how homeowners end up with a new opener and the same problem.

Opener repairs in your area start with isolating whether the fault is the door, the safety system or the unit itself. Our your county technicians service belt, chain and screw-drive openers, including Wi-Fi and battery-backup models.

What opener repair involves

A garage door opener is a motor with a trolley, rail, limit switch and safety system. Repairs cover the drive gear, capacitor, logic board, travel limits, force settings, safety sensors, wall console, remotes and the trolley connection to the door. Diagnosis is as much about the door's balance as it is about the electronics.

What is included

  • Full opener and door balance diagnosis
  • Gear, capacitor, board or trolley repair as needed
  • Safety sensor alignment, cleaning and rewiring
  • Remote and keypad reprogramming
  • Travel limit and force adjustment
  • Advice on repair versus replacement with straight pricing

Signs you need this service

  • The opener hums but the door does not move
  • The door opens a little and reverses, or stops halfway
  • The wall button works but the remote does not
  • The opener runs for a second and clicks off
  • The safety light blinks and the door refuses to close
  • Grinding or clicking from the opener housing

How the visit works

  1. 1

    Test the door first

    A door that is out of balance or binding will make any opener fail. We disconnect the opener and check the door by hand.

  2. 2

    Isolate the fault

    Sensors, wiring, wall console, remote and the unit's internal components are tested one by one.

  3. 3

    Repair what is broken

    Gears, capacitors, boards, sensors and remotes are replaced individually rather than swapping the entire unit.

  4. 4

    Recalibrate

    Travel limits and force settings are reset, remotes are paired and the safety reverse is tested.

Opener Repair questions

Is it worth repairing a 15-year-old opener?

If the repair is under roughly a third of the price of a new unit and the motor is quiet and strong, repair is usually worth it. Older units with obsolete boards, no safety reverse or failing motors are better replaced.

Why does my opener only work with the wall button?

That points to the remote, its battery, the receiver board or the programming rather than the motor. It is usually a quick fix.

Why does the door reverse as soon as it touches the floor?

The force setting is too low or the travel limit is misadjusted. It can also be a sign that the door is binding or the springs are losing tension.

Can you repair a Wi-Fi opener?

Yes. Connectivity issues are usually a router band problem, a firmware update or a failed logic board — all diagnosable on site.

My opener is beeping and the light flashes. What does that mean?

On most modern units that is a safety-sensor fault or a battery-backup warning. The technician reads the diagnostic code and fixes the cause rather than resetting it repeatedly.
