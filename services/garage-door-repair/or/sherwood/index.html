What we handle in Sherwood

Garage door repair covers everything between the door panels and the opener: torsion or extension springs, lift cables, rollers, hinges, bearings, tracks, drums, bottom brackets, weather seals and the safety system. A proper repair restores smooth, balanced travel — it is not just making the door open again.

  • Full 20-point inspection of door, hardware, tracks and opener
  • Written quote before any repair work starts
  • Spring, cable, roller, hinge and bracket replacement with rated parts
  • Track realignment and door balance adjustment
  • Safety sensor testing and travel-limit calibration
  • Post-repair operation test and lubrication of moving parts

When to call for door repair in Sherwood

  • The door opens a few inches and stops, or reverses without warning
  • A loud bang from the garage followed by a door that will not lift
  • Grinding, squealing or shuddering as the door moves
  • The door hangs crooked, or one side lifts before the other
  • The opener runs but the door does not move
  • Visible gaps at the bottom or daylight at the corners when closed

Local conditions that affect doors in Sherwood

In Washington County, the recurring repairs are rotten bottom rails, failed seals, rusty tracks and doors that swell and stick in the wettest months.

Rot in wooden doors

Persistent moisture attacks the bottom rail and joints first.

Rust on tracks and cables

Constant damp shortens the life of bare steel parts.

Failed bottom seals

Standing water at the apron destroys seals and lets rain into the garage.

Moss and algae

Growth on the door and apron traps moisture against the surface.

Timing note: Inspect the bottom rail and seals before the rainy season, and keep the apron clear so water drains away from the door.

Door choice: Fiberglass, aluminum or steel with a factory finish avoids the rot problem entirely in a wet climate.

Before the technician arrives: If the door is open and will not close, disconnect the opener with the release cord and lower the door by hand only if it moves freely. If it feels heavy or binds, leave it and call — a heavy door usually means a spring or cable has failed.

Is it cheaper to repair or replace my door?

If the panels, frame and hardware are sound, a repair is almost always cheaper. Replacement makes more sense when several sections are damaged, the door is more than 20 years old, or repairs are approaching the cost of a new door.

Yes. Opener motors, gears, logic boards, safety sensors and remotes are part of the same visit, so a door-and-opener problem is solved in one appointment.
