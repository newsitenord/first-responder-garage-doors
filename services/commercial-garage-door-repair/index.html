A commercial door that is down can stop loading, close a bay or leave a building unsecured, so commercial repair is measured in downtime as much as in parts. Roll-up doors, sectional overhead doors and high-cycle systems wear differently from residential doors and need parts rated for the number of cycles they actually run.

Commercial repair in your area covers warehouses, workshops, storage facilities, retail bays and agricultural buildings across your county. Technicians diagnose the door, the operator and the safety devices together, and prioritise getting the opening working or safely secured.

What commercial repair involves

Commercial garage door repair covers roll-up (coiling) doors, sectional overhead doors and their operators: springs and torsion assemblies, drums, chains, guides, slats, bottom bars, safety edges, photo-eyes and control panels. Commercial doors are built for higher cycle counts, so using residential-grade parts is a false economy.

What is included

  • Commercial door and operator diagnosis
  • Roll-up slat, guide and bottom-bar repair
  • Torsion spring and drum replacement for high-cycle use
  • Safety edge and photo-eye testing and repair
  • Operator control and limit adjustment
  • Downtime-first scheduling with clear pricing

Signs you need this service

  • The door will not open or close, blocking a bay
  • The roll-up curtain has jumped its guides
  • The operator runs but the door does not move
  • Slats are bent after a forklift or vehicle impact
  • The door is noisy, slow or stops partway
  • Safety edges or photo-eyes are faulting

How the visit works

  1. 1

Why homeowners call us instead of guessing

A garage door is a system: springs, cables, tracks, rollers, panels and the opener all affect each other. Replacing the part that broke without checking why it broke is how the same repair comes back a month later. Every dispatch starts with a diagnosis of the whole door, a written price and a test of the safety reverse before the technician leaves.

The local company carries the licensing and insurance for the work, and qualifying repairs are backed by a one-year workmanship warranty. If a repair is not worth doing, you will hear that too — replacement is quoted honestly rather than pushed.

Commercial Repair questions

Do you work outside business hours?

Yes, commercial repairs can be scheduled outside opening hours or during a planned shutdown to limit downtime.

Can you repair a roll-up door rather than replace it?

Usually yes. Individual slats, guides, bottom bars and springs can be replaced; full replacement is only needed when the curtain or structure is badly damaged.

Do you service operators as well as doors?

Yes. Control panels, limits, safety devices and operators are part of commercial service.

How fast can you attend a commercial breakdown in your area?

Commercial calls are prioritised. Same-day attendance is common in your county, with emergency dispatch where a bay is blocked.

Can you service multiple locations?

Yes. Multi-site property managers can set up scheduled maintenance across locations rather than calling for each breakdown.
