Most garage door failures are not sudden. Springs lose tension, rollers flat-spot, hinges dry out, tracks shift a fraction of an inch and force settings drift — and then one cold morning the door refuses to close. A tune-up catches those small changes while they are still cheap to fix.

A tune-up in your area takes about an hour and covers the door, the hardware and the opener. It is the single most cost-effective service for a garage door, and it is what we recommend before winter in your county.

What tune-up involves

A garage door tune-up is a preventive service, not a repair. It includes lubrication of springs, hinges, rollers and bearings; balance testing; track alignment; tightening of hardware; adjustment of opener travel and force limits; and testing of the safety reverse system. Anything found worn is quoted separately before it is replaced.

What is included

  • Lubrication of springs, hinges, rollers, bearings and tracks
  • Balance test with the opener disconnected
  • Track alignment and hardware tightening
  • Roller and hinge wear inspection
  • Safety reverse and photo-eye test
  • Opener travel and force adjustment

Signs you need this service

  • The door has not been serviced in more than a year
  • It sounds louder or rougher than it used to
  • The door shudders or hesitates when it starts to move
  • The opener's light blinks or the door reverses occasionally
  • You are about to sell or buy a house with a garage
  • Winter or storm season is coming

How the visit works

  1. 1

    Inspect

    The technician works through the door, hardware and opener, noting wear before touching any adjustment.

  2. 2

    Lubricate and adjust

    Moving parts are lubricated with the correct product, tracks are aligned and loose hardware is torqued.

  3. 3

    Balance and calibrate

    The door is balanced with the opener released, then the opener's travel and force settings are reset.

  4. 4

    Test and report

    Safety reverse is tested at the floor, and you get a written note of anything that will need attention soon.

Why homeowners call us instead of guessing

A garage door is a system: springs, cables, tracks, rollers, panels and the opener all affect each other. Replacing the part that broke without checking why it broke is how the same repair comes back a month later. Every dispatch starts with a diagnosis of the whole door, a written price and a test of the safety reverse before the technician leaves.

The local company carries the licensing and insurance for the work, and qualifying repairs are backed by a one-year workmanship warranty. If a repair is not worth doing, you will hear that too — replacement is quoted honestly rather than pushed.

Tune-Up questions

How often should a garage door be tuned up?

Once a year for most homes, and twice a year if the door gets heavy daily use or the climate swings hard between seasons.

Is a tune-up worth it if the door works fine?

Yes. The point is to catch worn rollers, loose hardware and weak springs before they fail and take a cable or opener with them.

What is actually included?

Lubrication, balance testing, track alignment, hardware tightening, safety-reverse testing and opener limit and force adjustment. Parts are quoted separately.

How long does it take?

About 45–90 minutes for a single door, depending on condition and how far out of adjustment it is.

Do you service doors you did not install?

Yes. Tune-ups are available for any brand of residential garage door and opener.
