Not sure what is wrong? Start with the symptom

  • Will not lift at all — usually a broken spring or snapped cable. Do not keep running the opener.
  • Lifts a few inches and stops — spring tension, travel limits or a binding track.
  • Crooked or hanging on one side — cable, roller or track failure. Stop using the door.
  • Opener hums but nothing moves — stripped gear, broken trolley or a door too heavy for the unit.
  • Reverses before touching the floor — safety sensor or force setting, sometimes a binding door.
  • Loud bang from the garage — a torsion spring has broken; treat it as urgent.

Emergency? Call first.

A door stuck open, hanging off the track or trapping a vehicle should not wait for a scheduled appointment.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know which service I need?

Start with the symptom: a door that will not lift usually points to springs or cables; a door that opens unevenly points to a cable, roller or track; an opener that hums without moving is an opener or balance problem. The dispatcher asks a few questions and routes the job to the right specialist.

Do all 16 services cover every city?

Coverage varies by market. Residential repair, installation, openers and tune-ups are available almost everywhere in the network; commercial and specialty work depends on the local partner. The dispatcher confirms availability for your address before booking.

Can two services be done in one visit?

Yes, and it is usually cheaper. A spring replacement with a tune-up, or a new door with an opener install, is one trip and one call-out fee instead of two.

What is the difference between repair and replacement?

Repair keeps the existing door and replaces failed parts. Replacement removes the door and its hardware and installs a new system. If several sections are damaged or the door is over 20 years old, replacement is usually the better value.
