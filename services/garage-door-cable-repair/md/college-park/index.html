What we handle in College Park

Garage doors use two lift cables, one on each side, wound around drums at the ends of the torsion shaft or connected to extension springs. They carry the door's weight in tension. Cables wear at the drum, at the bottom bracket and wherever they rub against a misaligned track — which is why a cable failure is often a symptom of another problem.

  • Cable pair replacement with correct gauge and length
  • Drum, bracket and track inspection
  • Spring tension check and rebalancing
  • Roller and hinge inspection on the affected side
  • Opener force reset after the repair
  • Full open-close safety test

When to call for cable repair in College Park

  • A cable hanging loose or visibly unwound from the drum
  • The door is crooked, or one corner is lower than the other
  • The door slams down instead of lowering smoothly
  • Frayed strands visible near the drum or bottom bracket
  • The door will not open at all after a loud snap
  • The bottom bracket is bent or pulled away from the panel

Local conditions that affect doors in College Park

Because Prince George's County also sees tropical storms and heavy rain events, we treat wind-rated hardware and a properly sealed perimeter as part of the job, not an upgrade.

Rust and corrosion

High humidity keeps bare steel damp; hinges, rollers and bottom brackets corrode quickly without regular lubrication.

Swollen or warped wood doors

Moisture absorption changes the shape of wooden panels and can jam them in the opening.

Seal and weatherstrip failure

UV exposure and heat make vinyl seals brittle, and the first storm pushes water straight in.

Storm wind pressure

Loose tracks, hinges and anchors are the first thing to fail in a high-wind event.

Timing note: Inspect and lubricate before hurricane season, and re-check the perimeter seals afterwards; storm-driven rain finds every gap.

Door choice: Choose a wind-rated door with factory-applied finish and stainless or galvanized hardware, and skip unfinished wood in exposed openings.

Before the technician arrives: Test the safety reverse monthly: place a roll of paper towels under the door and close it. If the door does not reverse on contact, the opener needs adjustment before someone gets hurt.

Cable Repair in College Park: common questions

Can I open the door with a broken cable?

No. With one cable gone the door is unsupported on that side and can drop or twist the panels. Keep it closed and call for service.

Why did my cable break?

The usual causes are a worn drum, a misaligned track rubbing the cable, corrosion from salt or humidity, or spring tension that was never balanced correctly.

Do both cables need replacing?

Yes, in almost every case. Both cables have carried the same load and worn at the same rate; replacing one leaves the other close to failure.
