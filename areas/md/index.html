Local conditions across Maryland

Maryland sits in a humid coastal climate where summer moisture, storm winds and salt air all reach the garage. Steel parts rust from the inside out, wooden sections swell, and seals that look fine in spring can let water in by the end of summer.

Because Maryland also sees tropical storms and heavy rain events, we treat wind-rated hardware and a properly sealed perimeter as part of the job, not an upgrade.

What we see most in Maryland

  • Rust and corrosion. High humidity keeps bare steel damp; hinges, rollers and bottom brackets corrode quickly without regular lubrication.
  • Swollen or warped wood doors. Moisture absorption changes the shape of wooden panels and can jam them in the opening.
  • Seal and weatherstrip failure. UV exposure and heat make vinyl seals brittle, and the first storm pushes water straight in.
  • Storm wind pressure. Loose tracks, hinges and anchors are the first thing to fail in a high-wind event.
  • Opener electronics and moisture. Humidity and power surges after storms are both hard on circuit boards and safety sensors.

Seasonal advice

Inspect and lubricate before hurricane season, and re-check the perimeter seals afterwards; storm-driven rain finds every gap.

Choose a wind-rated door with factory-applied finish and stainless or galvanized hardware, and skip unfinished wood in exposed openings.
