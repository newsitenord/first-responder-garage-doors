Local conditions across Minnesota

Minnesota sees long stretches below freezing and heavy snowfall. Snow and ice pile up against the door, and the opener is asked to move a door that is heavier than usual because of snow load, stiff grease and a frozen seal.

Ice dams and packed snow at the apron also block the door from closing properly, which leaves a gap and lets cold air and meltwater into the garage. In Minnesota, the repairs we see most are snapped springs, burned-out opener gears and bent bottom sections.

What we see most in Minnesota

  • Snow load on panels. Heavy snow pressed against the door can bow a steel panel or crack a wood section.
  • Frozen bottom seal. Ice at the apron holds the door up and the opener keeps running against a closed limit.
  • Spring breakage in deep cold. The coldest weeks are when torsion and extension springs fail most often.
  • Opener overload. Cold-thickened lubricant and heavier door travel strain the motor, gears and belt.
  • Roof and carport snow load. Doors under carports or low roofs can be blocked by snow sliding off the structure.

Seasonal advice

Clear snow and ice from the apron before operating the door, and have the spring and balance checked every autumn before the snow arrives.

A well-insulated steel door with heavy-duty hardware and a modern DC opener handles snow-belt winters far better than a lightweight uninsulated door.
